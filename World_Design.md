# NEON ASHES — WORLD DESIGN

## 0. PURPOSE

This document defines the physical, social, economic, visual, and systemic design of **Vespera City**, the open world of NEON ASHES.

The city must feel like a believable living metropolis rather than a collection of disconnected gameplay zones.

The player should be able to travel through the city without constantly thinking:

> "This area exists because there is a mission here."

The world itself should provide reasons to explore.

---

# 1. WORLD IDENTITY

## City

**Vespera City**

## Country

The fictional country of **Aurelia**.

## Population

Approximately 6.5 million people in the metropolitan region.

## Climate

Warm coastal climate.

Characteristics:

- humid summers
- mild winters
- frequent coastal clouds
- occasional heavy rain
- thunderstorms
- fog near the harbor
- bright sunny afternoons
- hazy mornings

## Geography

Vespera is built around:

- a large natural harbor
- a central bay
- coastal beaches
- low hills
- dense urban development
- industrial waterfront
- suburban outskirts

The city should have noticeable elevation changes.

Some roads should climb hills.

Some districts should sit significantly higher than others.

---

# 2. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

The world must satisfy five principles.

## 2.1 BELIEVABILITY

Buildings, roads, businesses and neighborhoods should appear to exist for believable reasons.

## 2.2 VARIETY

The player should be able to travel for several minutes without seeing the exact same environment repeatedly.

## 2.3 CONNECTIVITY

Districts should naturally connect.

Avoid artificial boundaries.

## 2.4 DENSITY

Downtown should feel dense.

Industrial areas should feel spacious.

Suburbs should feel quieter.

## 2.5 DISCOVERY

The player should regularly encounter interesting things without being forced into missions.

---

# 3. CITY LAYOUT

Vespera City is divided into ten major districts:

1. Central Vesper
2. Old Harbor
3. Eastline
4. Redwater
5. Meridian
6. North Heights
7. Silver Coast
8. Southside
9. Industrial Belt
10. Outer County

Each district should have smaller neighborhoods.

---

## Identity

The financial and commercial heart of the city.

## Visual characteristics

- skyscrapers
- glass office towers
- corporate plazas
- hotels
- restaurants
- shopping streets
- underground parking
- dense traffic
- pedestrian-heavy sidewalks

However, do NOT overuse glass.

The visual language should mix:

- concrete
- stone
- steel
- brick
- glass

Buildings should have different ages.

## Major landmarks

### Vesper Tower

The tallest building in the city.

### Meridian Exchange

Large financial complex.

### Civic Plaza

Public square containing government buildings.

### Grand Vesper Hotel

Luxury hotel.

### Central Station

Major train station.

## Gameplay

Central Vesper is useful for:

- corporate missions
- investigations
- surveillance
- vehicle pursuits
- social encounters
- high-value stores

## NPC behavior

Day:

Very busy.

Night:

Restaurants, bars, hotels and nightlife remain active.

- local businesses

# 14. ROAD NETWORK

The city needs several road categories.

## Highways

Fast travel between major areas.

Should include:

- interchanges
- exits
- bridges
- tunnels
- service roads

## Major boulevards

Wide roads through dense urban areas.

## Local roads

Smaller streets.

## Alleys

Narrow spaces useful for:

- shortcuts
- hiding
- missions
- exploration

## Rural roads

Outer County.

## Mountain roads

North Heights.

Roads should not form a perfect grid.

Real cities have irregular roads.

---

# 15. MAJOR BRIDGES

Create at least three major bridges.

### Vesper Bridge

Connects downtown with eastern districts.

### Harbor Bridge

Connects Old Harbor and Southside.

### Silver Span

Large coastal bridge.

Each bridge should be visually recognizable.

---

# 16. PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION

Implement simplified public transport where feasible.

Systems:

- trains
- buses
- taxis

The player does not necessarily need to control public transportation.

It exists to make the city feel alive.

---

# 17. TRAFFIC

Traffic density should depend on:

- district
- time
- weather
- story progression
- road type

### Morning

Heavy commuter traffic.

### Afternoon

Moderate traffic.

### Evening

Heavy traffic.

### Late night

Reduced traffic.

Industrial districts should have more trucks.

Tourist districts should have more taxis.

Wealthier districts should have more expensive vehicles.

---

# 18. PEDESTRIANS

Pedestrian populations should vary.

Central Vesper:

Business workers.

Silver Coast:

Tourists.

Eastline:

Residents.

Industrial Belt:

Workers.

North Heights:

Residents and service workers.

Outer County:

Low density.

Pedestrians should have simple routines.

---

# 19. BUSINESSES

Populate the city with functional or visually convincing businesses.

Examples:

- convenience stores
- restaurants
- cafes
- clothing stores
- vehicle garages
- supermarkets
- bars
- clubs
- hotels
- electronics shops
- pharmacies
- gas stations

Not every building needs to be enterable.

Prioritize meaningful interiors.

---

# 20. GAS STATIONS

Gas stations should be recognizable landmarks.

They can include:

- fuel pumps
- convenience store
- parking area
- car wash

If fuel mechanics are implemented, keep them optional.

Do NOT force repetitive refueling.

---

# 21. GARAGES

Garages are important gameplay locations.

The player can use them for:

- vehicle repair
- customization
- vehicle storage
- changing vehicles

Jonah's garage should be the primary early-game garage.

---

# 22. SAFEHOUSES

Safehouses should be distributed throughout the world.

Each should have a different identity.

Examples:

- small Eastline apartment
- warehouse hideout
- suburban house
- luxury apartment

Safehouses unlock through story progression.

---

# 23. INTERIOR DESIGN

Interiors should use believable layouts.

Avoid:

"door → giant empty room → mission arena."

Apartments should have:

- bedrooms
- bathrooms
- kitchens
- living rooms

Businesses should have:

- counters
- storage
- staff areas
- bathrooms where appropriate

---

# 24. LANDMARK DESIGN

Every major district needs memorable landmarks.

Landmarks should help the player navigate without constantly checking the map.

Examples:

- towers
- bridges
- stadiums
- monuments
- distinctive buildings
- hills
- cranes
- signs

---

# 25. VERTICALITY

The world should have meaningful vertical design.

Examples:

- parking garages
- rooftops
- staircases
- fire escapes
- underground areas
- bridges
- elevated highways
- hills

However, do not make every building climbable.

---

# 26. HIDDEN LOCATIONS

Create hidden areas.

Examples:

- abandoned tunnel
- hidden garage
- rooftop
- underground room
- abandoned factory office
- forest cabin
- secret dock
- maintenance tunnel

Hidden locations can contain:

- collectibles
- money
- evidence
- weapons
- environmental storytelling

---

# 27. WEATHER EFFECTS ON THE WORLD

Rain should affect the appearance and behavior of the city.

During rain:

- pedestrians use umbrellas if implemented
- traffic becomes slightly slower
- roads become reflective
- puddles appear
- visibility decreases
- certain NPC activities change

Heavy rain should create a more dramatic atmosphere.

Fog should especially affect:

- harbor
- hills
- rural outskirts

---

# 28. TIME-BASED WORLD CHANGES

Locations should change based on time.

Example:

A street may be:

Morning:
commuters.

Afternoon:
shops active.

Evening:
restaurants busy.

Night:
clubs and bars active.

Late night:
mostly traffic and nightlife workers.

This should be achieved through spawning/despawning and behavior changes rather than requiring separate maps.

---

# 29. FACTION TERRITORIES

Factions should have recognizable areas of influence.

## Marrow Syndicate

Primarily:

- Old Harbor
- Southside
- portions of Industrial Belt

## Eastside Crew

Primarily:

- Eastline
- parts of Redwater

## Vespera Strategic Security

Primarily:

- corporate areas
- wealthy districts
- secured facilities

## Police

City-wide presence.

Faction influence should evolve during the story.

---

# 30. WORLD REACTIVITY

The world should respond to story progression.

Examples:

After a major gang conflict:

- increased police presence
- damaged vehicles
- graffiti
- news reports
- changed NPC dialogue

After redevelopment:

- demolished buildings
- construction sites
- new buildings

After a major corporate scandal:

- protests
- news vans
- increased security

---

# 31. RANDOM WORLD EVENTS

Random events should spawn naturally.

Possible events:

- car crash
- robbery
- police pursuit
- street fight
- suspicious exchange
- vehicle breakdown
- pedestrian requesting help
- gang confrontation
- illegal race
- police checkpoint

Events should have probabilities rather than appearing constantly.

---

# 32. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING

The city should communicate its history visually.

Examples:

Old buildings beside new towers.

Demolished homes beside luxury developments.

Memorials.

Old advertisements.

Graffiti.

Construction signs.

Political posters.

Abandoned businesses.

These details should reinforce the themes of inequality, redevelopment and corruption.

# 34. LIGHTING

Lighting should communicate district identity.

Central Vesper:

Bright commercial lighting.

Eastline:

Mixed street lighting.

Old Harbor:

Industrial lighting.

Silver Coast:

Neon nightlife.

North Heights:

Warm residential lighting.

Industrial Belt:

Harsh industrial lighting.

Outer County:

Dark rural roads.

---

# 35. COLOR AND MATERIAL PHILOSOPHY

The game should NOT use a generic "everything is neon blue and purple" aesthetic.

Neon exists primarily in:

- nightlife
- advertisements
- clubs
- entertainment areas

Normal neighborhoods should use:

- concrete
- brick
- asphalt
- painted metal
- glass
- stone
- wood

The overall world should feel grounded.

---

# 36. NO GLASSMORPHISM

This rule applies specifically to the game's UI and visual interface.

Do NOT use:

- excessive transparent panels
- blurry glass cards
- floating glass rectangles
- translucent gradient dashboards
- futuristic glass interfaces

The game should feel like a premium console/PC game rather than an AI-generated SaaS dashboard.

Menus should use:

- solid surfaces
- strong contrast
- clean typography
- subtle borders
- restrained shadows
- deliberate spacing

---

# 37. MAP PHILOSOPHY

The map should communicate geography.

Do not make the map look like a generic web-app map.

It should resemble an actual game map.

Show:

- roads
- major landmarks
- district boundaries
- discovered locations
- mission locations
- safehouses
- shops
- garages

Undiscovered locations should remain hidden or minimally marked.

---

# 38. EXPLORATION REWARDS

Exploration should have tangible rewards.

Possible discoveries:

- hidden cash
- unique vehicles
- weapons
- evidence
- character encounters
- shortcuts
- secret locations
- collectibles
- lore

Do not fill the map with hundreds of meaningless collectibles.

Quality over quantity.

---

# 39. COLLECTIBLES

Use a small number of collectible categories.

Examples:

### Adrian's Records

Pieces of Adrian's history.

### Black Ledger Evidence

Pieces of the conspiracy.

### Vespera Memories

Environmental photographs.

Collectibles should reveal information rather than simply increase a counter.

---

# 40. WORLD ECONOMY

Different districts should have different economic profiles.

Central Vesper:

High prices.

North Heights:

Very high prices.

Silver Coast:

Tourist prices.

Eastline:

Affordable.

Southside:

Affordable.

Outer County:

Cheap but limited selection.

This should influence shops and services where practical.

---

# 41. POLICE PRESENCE

Police density varies by district.

Central Vesper:

High.

North Heights:

High.

Eastline:

Moderate.

Industrial Belt:

Moderate.

Outer County:

Low.

Police should patrol logically.

---

# 42. SECURITY LEVEL

Certain areas should naturally have greater security.

Examples:

Corporate buildings.

Government buildings.

Luxury estates.

Private security facilities.

Ports.

This can affect mission difficulty.

---

# 43. WORLD ACCESSIBILITY

The player should NOT receive access to every meaningful location immediately.

Story progression can unlock:

- safehouses
- restricted interiors
- faction areas
- certain facilities
- special activities

However, the city itself should remain largely explorable from the beginning.

Do not artificially lock entire districts unless there is a strong narrative reason.

---

# 44. WORLD STREAMING

If the game is implemented as a browser game, the world should be divided into manageable chunks.

Only nearby:

- NPCs
- vehicles
- physics objects
- effects
- interactive objects

should remain fully active.

Distant objects can use simplified representations.

The player should never notice obvious loading boundaries if technically possible.

---

# 45. WORLD POPULATION

Do not attempt to simulate every citizen individually.

Use intelligent population management.

Example:

Near player:

High-detail NPC simulation.

Medium distance:

Simplified simulation.

Far distance:

Visual-only traffic/pedestrians or no simulation.

This keeps the game performant.

---

# 46. DISTRICT TRANSITIONS

District boundaries should feel natural.

Examples:

Central Vesper → Meridian:

Buildings become shorter.

Meridian → Eastline:

Architecture becomes older and denser.

Old Harbor → Industrial Belt:

Warehouses become larger.

Silver Coast → North Heights:

Tourist development transitions into luxury housing.

City → Outer County:

Buildings gradually disappear.

---

# 47. WORLD EVENTS DURING STORY

Story missions should be able to temporarily modify the world.

Examples:

- police lockdown
- road closure
- faction takeover
- protest
- construction shutdown
- increased security
- damaged building
- abandoned district

These changes can be temporary or permanent.

---

# 48. PERMANENT WORLD CHANGES

Major story decisions can permanently change selected locations.

Examples:

A nightclub may close.

A business may reopen.

A faction may lose territory.

A building may be destroyed.

A character may move.

A police presence may increase.

Keep permanent changes limited and meaningful.

---

# 49. PLAYER FREEDOM

The player should be able to ignore the main story and explore.

The world must remain fun without mission objectives.

Examples:

Drive across the city.

Find a hidden location.

Customize a vehicle.

Race an NPC.

Visit a restaurant.

Explore the harbor.

Watch the city during a storm.

Discover an unusual NPC encounter.

---

# 50. WORLD DESIGN QUALITY BAR

The world should make the player want to ask:

> "What's over there?"

rather than:

> "Where is the next mission marker?"

The best areas should have:

- visual identity
- gameplay purpose
- narrative purpose
- believable infrastructure
- ambient life
- secrets
- memorable landmarks

Vespera City should feel like a place where people actually live.

---

# 51. IMPLEMENTATION PRIORITY

If technical limitations prevent the entire city from being built simultaneously, prioritize:

### Tier 1

Eastline

Old Harbor

Central Vesper

### Tier 2

Southside

Meridian

Industrial Belt

### Tier 3

North Heights

Silver Coast

Redwater

### Tier 4

Outer County

Additional interiors

Advanced world simulation

The player should receive a coherent, interconnected city rather than ten unfinished districts.

---

# 52. FINAL WORLD PRINCIPLE

Vespera City is not merely the location of NEON ASHES.

**Vespera City IS part of the story.**

Its wealth, poverty, redevelopment, criminal activity, architecture, infrastructure, businesses, people, police presence, and changing neighborhoods should gradually reveal the world that Kaleb is fighting against.

The player should understand the Black Ledger partly because they understand how Vespera itself works.

The city should tell its own story even when Kaleb is standing completely still.
